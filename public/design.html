Digital Duo · design language · proposed 26 Sep 2026

The duo always writes behind a line.

Every screen in Digital Duo carries two voices: a person, and that person's duo. Hairline exists to keep those voices impossible to confuse, and to keep the evidence that connects them one glance away. It takes its name from the confidence hairline, the 2px line on a draft's leading edge. That line is the one element the product already ships that nobody else has, so Hairline makes it the signature.

Source of truth: duo/docs/design-language.md. It extends client-design.md, the Part 4 tokens and vocabulary.md, and never overrides them.

01 · Principles

Six principles, each with a test

P1

Attributed, always

Generated text sits behind the hairline and under an attribution line. Nothing else uses a leading line.

Test: cover the words. You can still tell which blocks the duo wrote.

P2

Why costs zero clicks

Provenance sits on screen by default, in a rail on desktop and a drawer on phone. It is never a disclosure triangle.

Test: a screenshot of any draft answers "why did it say that?"

P3

Quiet until it matters

Greyscale, plus one accent that means action available. There is one count, and attention is batched.

Test: squint. The only colour is what's waiting on you.

P4

Decisions, not settings

Trust changes only when the owner answers a concrete example: an offer, a rule, a grant in plain words.

Test: no switch anywhere changes what the duo may do.

P5

Cautious by design

Promotions are offers and demotions are announcements. Irreversible acts take two steps, with Cancel focused.

Test: every T4 path has two screens and no focused primary.

P6

Keyboard-first, touch-complete

One key per verb in every queue. Every verb also has a 44px control on phone.

Test: clear Review without a mouse, then clear it one-handed.

06 · Interaction patterns

Eleven patterns

5.1TriageSuperhuman · Linear Triage

Review is a queue with a count, and it opens on the oldest undecided item. J/K moves, ⏎ sends, E edits, X ignores, and O opens the conversation.

Ignore can be undone for 5s because nothing has left the machine. Send can't be undone, and the UI doesn't pretend otherwise. The empty state is "Review is clear.", with no confetti.

5.2Edit to teachGitHub suggested changes

Editing happens in place with the hairline kept. Send edited (⌘⏎) shows a toast that links to Corrections, where the edit renders as a diff with the proposed rule beneath it.

5.3Approve a draft of yourselfSpotify Wrapped

The Mirror opens on a portrait of three to five serif sentences built from the owner's own counts. Rule cards follow one at a time, with evidence. A approves and R rejects. Progress reads "4 of 12", never a percentage.

5.4Offer and announceApple permission prompts

A promotion is an offer card in Review, beside the draft that earned it. A demotion is a sentence with no buttons, in the attribution line and in Audit.

5.5Deliberate confirm

The card states what, to what, and whether it can be undone. Review and confirm… opens a dialog that restates it with Cancel focused. It is disabled offline and never queued.

5.6Escalation as a question

An escalation shows the question, its reason, and a composer. The answer becomes a standing-order candidate. A refusal has no actions.

5.7Two searches, never mergedRaycast

/ is Search what was said, across the workspace. ⌘K is Search what your duo knows, which is private. Neither one falls back to the other.

5.8Offline honesty

Messages queue while offline. Decisions (Send, Keep, Confirm) are disabled, with the reason shown, and never queued.

5.9Batched attention

The badge shows the Review count only. Only urgent escalations interrupt. Do not disturb holds the duo's acts too, and they collect in "what was missed".

5.10Ending things

Forget this device, Revoke, and End this duo (typed END) are three verbs for three acts. Each one says what ends, what is kept, and what can't be undone.

5.11Empty states teach

Every empty state says what will fill it and how, and never offers a fake action. For example: "Nothing earned yet. As your duo answers and you correct it, topics it handles well will show up here."

08 · Information architecture

Three groups, one spine

The shipped sidebar lists twelve surfaces as peers. The proposal groups them in the order of the owner's morning: decide, talk, tune.

duoYour duo ▾
Review 3 Corrections 2 Confirmations 1 Conversations Anaproject-xBose's duo Your duo AskMirrorKnowledgeTrustEscalations
AuditChannelsSettings
1

Group into Inbox, Conversations, Your duo and a footer. Group headings are labels, not links.

2

Rename "Duo" to "Ask". The vocabulary already owns the verb ("Ask your duo…"), and "Duo" collides with the roster above it. Keep the #duo hash.

3

Hide Confirmations at zero. It's a queue, not a place.

4

Move Channels to the footer. Connecting happens elsewhere, and the owner visits only to learn why something is quiet.

5

Keep Mirror and Knowledge apart. Mirror is who it thinks you are. Knowledge is what it has read.

6

The phone folds the same map. Review stays on top, the rail becomes a half-open drawer, and there is no tab bar.

09 · Voice

Plain, exact, attributed

Plain and exact

"Your duo refused this one." No "Oops!", no apologies, no exclamation marks.

First person about itself only

"I got that wrong, so I've gone back to asking you first." The duo never speaks in the first person as the owner.

The daemon's own counts

"23 times in six months", never "a lot". Proportions are shown as bar widths, never percentages.

Never a person

Never an employee, assistant, teammate or clone. The nouns and verbs are in vocabulary.md.
